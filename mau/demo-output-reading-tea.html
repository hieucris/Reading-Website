The Story of Tea

How a Chinese legend became the world's favourite drink

Tea is the most popular drink in the world after water. It is made from the leaves of the Camellia sinensis plant, which first grew in the forests of south-west China. According to legend, the Chinese emperor Shen Nong discovered tea in 2737 BC when some leaves fell into his pot of boiling water.

For centuries, tea was used in China mainly as a medicine. It was not until the Tang dynasty that drinking tea became a social activity enjoyed by ordinary people. Tea houses appeared in towns and cities, and poets wrote about the pleasure of drinking tea with friends.

Tea reached Europe in the early 17th century, brought by Dutch traders. At first it was so expensive that only the rich could afford it, and it was kept in locked boxes. By the 18th century, however, prices had fallen and tea had become the national drink of Britain.

Today, more than three million tonnes of tea are produced every year. The largest producers are China and India, but tea is also grown in countries such as Kenya and Sri Lanka. Although coffee has become more popular in some countries, tea remains an important part of daily life for billions of people.
